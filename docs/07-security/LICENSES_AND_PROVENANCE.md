# Licenses, Intellectual Property, and Provenance

## Required questions for every dependency/content source

- Who owns it and where did it originate?
- What exact license or terms apply to code, assets, models, datasets, fonts, samples, plugins and output?
- Is commercial use allowed? Modification? Redistribution? Public release? Derivatives?
- Are attribution, notice, source disclosure, share-alike, copyleft, patent, or usage restrictions triggered?
- Does a model license differ from its training dataset or code license?
- Are outputs guaranteed, conditioned, or not addressed by terms?
- Is the asset a third-party likeness, trademark, copyrighted character, or other protected content?

## Rules

- Do not copy proprietary art, music, code, fonts, models, or assets into a shipped product without authorization.
- Record license and origin at ingestion, not only at release.
- Preserve license files and notices in dependencies and asset packages.
- Do not treat public access as a license.
- Do not assume every generative model permits commercial use or redistribution.
- Flag uncertain rights and stop release until resolved.
- Maintain a license bill of materials for all third-party code and content.
- Clearly distinguish generated, human-made, third-party, and derived material.

## Provenance of generated assets

Capture model/revision/hash, workflow version, prompt/instructions, seed, control images/geometry, adapters/LoRAs, upscalers, postprocessing, human edits, application versions, and source references where relevant. Preserve original inputs in protected project storage when authorized.
