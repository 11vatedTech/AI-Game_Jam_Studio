# Architecture, Spatial, Product, and Industrial Design

## Scope

Architectural concepts, floor plans, spatial layouts, interiors/exteriors, landscape, BIM coordination, product form, ergonomics, packaging, furniture, industrial design, mechanical assemblies, materials, fabrication, documentation and visualization.

## Tool classes

Evaluate Blender and FreeCAD for open/local 3D/CAD; OpenSCAD for scriptable parametric geometry; BIM-capable systems and professional architecture/CAD suites where required; procedural tools such as Houdini where they offer meaningful advantage. CAD/BIM data may need specialized formats and plugins beyond mesh exchange.

## Requirements

- Identify whether output is conceptual visualization, editable 3D model, parametric CAD, BIM model, construction documentation, manufacturing-ready geometry, or a mix.
- Record units, scale, tolerances, coordinate systems, levels, constraints, material assumptions, loads/safety assumptions if applicable, and governing codes only when supplied/verified.
- Preserve feature history and structured building/product metadata when requested.
- Distinguish visualization-grade geometry from engineering-valid geometry.
- Include dimensioned drawings, sections, assemblies, part lists or fabrication data when required.
- Validate manufacturability, wall thickness, clearances, fit, accessibility and assembly constraints for the declared process.
- Do not present an AI visualization as a stamped architectural or engineering document. Professional sign-off is required for regulated safety-critical work.

## Visual review

Composition, circulation, proportion, scale, lighting, materials, ergonomics, assembly logic, human factors, context, realism and presentation. Visual appeal cannot replace structural or code compliance.
