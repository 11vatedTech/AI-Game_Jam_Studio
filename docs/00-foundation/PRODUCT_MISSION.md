# Product Mission and North Star

## Mission

Create a universal creative-production intelligence: one agent that can understand incomplete human intent, expand it using professional knowledge, select and operate the most suitable tools, coordinate specialist workflows, manufacture genuine artifacts, assemble a coherent deliverable, and prove its quality.

## North-star experience

A user can describe a desired outcome in natural language, provide references and constraints, and receive a complete professional result—not merely code fragments, generated images, or a list of suggestions. The system should be able to research what the user did not know to ask, identify hidden dependencies, select an appropriate medium and production pipeline, make coherent design decisions, execute in real applications, critique the result, repair problems, and deliver the final work with evidence.

## Product pillars

1. **Universal intent understanding** — convert vague, incomplete, contradictory, or evolving requests into traceable requirements and assumptions.
2. **Professional domain intelligence** — understand craft-specific techniques, terminology, standards, constraints, and review criteria.
3. **Best-of-breed tool orchestration** — use professional applications and proven projects rather than replace them with superficial custom substitutes.
4. **Real production** — create editable, importable, runnable, renderable, and distributable artifacts in their native formats.
5. **Cross-discipline coherence** — ensure code, art, UX, sound, animation, narrative, performance, and branding feel like one intentional product.
6. **Visual and sensory excellence** — deliver distinctive art direction and polished outputs rather than generic defaults.
7. **Self-critique and repair** — inspect real outputs, locate root causes, iterate, and re-run impacted tests.
8. **Trust and evidence** — every completion claim is backed by actual artifacts, tests, application/runtime evidence, provenance, and a transparent report.
9. **Extensibility** — add new applications, models, formats, agents, and disciplines without rewriting the core.
10. **Economic control** — prioritize local, free, open systems while allowing explicit optional commercial tools where they materially raise quality.

## Success is not tool count

The system succeeds when it can complete high-value workflows end-to-end with demonstrable quality, not when it exposes the largest number of tool calls. Capability breadth without reliability, coherent outputs, security, or quality is not a competitive advantage.

## Scope universe

The initial scope includes games and interactive experiences; 2D/3D visual art; design and branding; CAD and product/industrial design; materials and rendering; rigging, animation, and mocap; VFX and simulation; audio, music, and voice; video and cinematography; UI/UX, websites, and applications; writing, narrative, and publishing; software engineering; data/scientific/technical computing; content packaging, deployment, and operations. The architecture must permit future disciplines without assuming they are already supported.

## User relationship

The agent is a capable production partner, not an unquestionable authority. It should challenge weak ideas constructively, expose tradeoffs, preserve user intent, seek authorization for consequential actions, and present evidence so the user can inspect and accept the work.
